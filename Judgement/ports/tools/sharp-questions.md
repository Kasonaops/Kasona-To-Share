# Tool: Sharp questions

## What it is

Not a session format. A short backlog of individual questions that tend to produce a concrete,
honest answer where a status question produces a polite one. Pull one or two that fit. Never
fire the list like a questionnaire.

## Reach for it when

You are preparing a conversation (a hire, a kickoff, a mentor call, a partner call, an
interview), or you are inside a session and your own answer to "what do you actually want out of
this" is vague.

## The questions

**1. The future-retrospective question.**
"Suppose this went really well, and a year from now you look back. What would have to have
happened for you to call it a good decision? What will I have done?"

Reframes "any questions for us" from a status signal into a request for success criteria. Use it:
- On yourself, as an outcome-ranking probe when your answer to "what do I want" is vague.
- Before writing a job spec: "a year from now this hire looks like a good decision. What did they
  actually do?"
- At a kickoff, in place of "what are your expectations".

Failure mode: asking it as a flourish and not using the answer. The whole value is that the answer
is concrete. Write it down. It becomes the success criteria.

**2. The wrong-assumption question.**
"What do people in this field keep getting wrong, and what do most of them simply not
understand?" Surfaces myths and sharp statements.

**3. The earlier-self question.**
"Knowing what you know now, what advice would you give yourself when you started?" Surfaces
experience-based insight and builds closeness.

**4. The real-problem question.**
"What problem did you originally set out to solve, and what turned out to be the real problem?"
Surfaces the insight behind the work.

**5. The one-thing question.**
"What is the one thing that matters most right now?" and, right after it, "what is the biggest
unknown?" Two questions that separate signal from noise fast.

**6. The good-but-annoying question.**
"What is good for you but sucks, and how could you make it better?"

**7. The explain-it-to-a-beginner format.**
Ask someone who is exceptionally good at something to teach it to an outsider. Produces clearer
explanations than asking them to explain it to a peer.

## What a bad application looks like

Firing several at once. Asking one and then not listening to the answer. Using question 1 with
someone who has no basis to answer it.

## Where its output lands in the Decision Record

**Ranked outcomes** (question 1 on yourself), **Missing information** (any question that
surfaces a gap), or the success criteria of a hire or a project.
