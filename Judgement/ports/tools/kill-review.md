# Tool: Kill review

## What it is

The strongest possible case *against* something that already exists, argued by a prosecutor
whose job is to take it apart, followed by a defence from the sponsor and a verdict. It is the
tool for killing or retiring: a product line, a channel, an offer, a tier, a content format, a
positioning, a recurring commitment on your own calendar.

## Reach for it when

Something already running might need to die. Not for a commitment you have not made yet (use a
pre-mortem for that), and not for something you have already decided to kill, where it turns
into a permission slip.

## Steps

1. **Name the thing and what it costs to keep**: budget, attention, calendar, people, focus.
2. **Prosecution.** Build the case using the fifteen reasons below. A decision to kill must map
   to at least one named reason. Otherwise it is a mood.
3. **Defence.** The sponsor answers each reason with evidence, not with loyalty.
4. **Verdict**, one of three:
   - **Keep with conditions:** name the conditions and a review date.
   - **Shrink:** keep it, cut its share of budget, roadmap or calendar.
   - **Retire:** stop it, and say what happens to the people and customers attached to it.
5. **Record it** with the bias check: sunk cost and loss aversion are the two most likely to
   have written the defence.

## The fifteen reasons

*A. The reason it exists is gone*

| # | Reason | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The original premise broke | The problem was solved elsewhere, the buyer changed, the channel died, or a platform shift made it structurally irrelevant |
| 2 | You cannot trust its numbers | Attribution is broken, the result does not survive a second look, or it is only defensible with a metric nobody can reproduce |
| 3 | A bolt-on ruined it | A feature, format or partner pulled it away from what it was, and the combined version serves nobody fully |

*B. Exhaustion and alternatives*

| # | Reason | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | It worked, and there is no second act | Flat growth, no next version anyone wants to build, the ceiling is visible and close |
| 5 | The people it depended on are gone | The only person who could run it left or checked out. It was never a system, only a person |
| 6 | The promise ran ahead of the delivery | It is priced, positioned or marketed past what it can do, and closing the gap costs more than it earns |

*C. Portfolio management*

| # | Reason | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | It takes too large a share | Too much roadmap, budget or calendar for what it returns. The concentration rule applied to attention |
| 8 | You lost interest, and it needs your interest | Legitimate for anything creative or founder-voiced. Not legitimate for an operational system that runs without you |
| 9 | It got absorbed | A platform, partner or default now does it natively and the reason people came to you is gone |
| 10 | You need the capacity elsewhere | A bigger, better-evidenced commitment needs the same hours, budget or person |
| 11 | Retiring it is worth more than running it | Killing it frees a price point, a positioning or a story you cannot tell while it exists |

*D. Systematic and values-based*

| # | Reason | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | A better alternative exists for the same slot | Same audience, same job, higher expected return, lower cost to run. The opportunity-cost reason |
| 13 | A hard rule broke | A floor set in advance was crossed: margin, churn, support load, delivery time, error rate. Counts only if the floor was written down *before* it broke |
| 14 | It stopped matching what you want to be known for | Values and identity, not economics. A profitable line can fail this one |
| 15 | It costs sleep or credibility disproportionately | The stress or the reputational drag is real even when the numbers look fine |

## What a bad application looks like

- Running the prosecution on something already decided, so the tool becomes a permission slip.
- Using reason 8 ("lost interest") to retire an operational system that does not need your
  interest at all.
- A verdict of "retire" with no answer for the customers, team or family attached to the thing.
  You cannot exit a relationship at a market price.
- A prosecutor who argues weak reasons because the strong one is uncomfortable to say aloud.

## Where its output lands in the Decision Record

**Options** (keep, shrink, retire, each with consequences), **Decision** (the verdict), and
**Rejected options** (the two verdicts not taken, with revisit triggers).
