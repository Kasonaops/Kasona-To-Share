# Tool: Next, Best, Affordable

## What it is

A three-question screen for a new idea, before it earns a full decision process. It is a gate,
not a debate. It comes from Josh Snow's talk about building and selling a consumer brand,
in an interview with Ryan Clogg (August 2025). Only this one reusable shape is taken from it.

1. Is it the **next** thing, or is it too early?
2. Is it the **best** version of that thing available right now?
3. Can you **afford** it in attention, cash and people, without starving what is already running?

## Reach for it when

A new idea shows up and the real question is whether to act on it *now*, not whether it is good.
It replaces vague prioritisation language ("it feels important") with three answerable
questions. It sits upstream of the full process: it decides whether the idea is worth running
through OOC/EMR at all. It does not replace OOC/EMR.

## Steps

1. Write the idea in one sentence.
2. Answer each question in one or two sentences, with the reason. A bare "yes" does not count.
   - **Next:** what has to be true first, and is it? What breaks if this comes in six months?
   - **Best:** what is the strongest version available now (a tool, a person, a design, a
     price), and is this it, or is it the first thing that came to mind?
   - **Affordable:** what does it cost in attention, cash and people, and what running thing
     gets less because of it? Name it.
3. Verdict: **go** (all three yes, run the full session), **park** (name a revisit trigger,
   observable, with a date), or **drop** (say why in one sentence).

## What a bad application looks like

- Answering "affordable" only in cash. Attention and people are the costs that actually starve
  what is running.
- Using "not next" as a permanent no. A park without a revisit trigger is an unspoken rejection.
- Running the screen on something you have already decided to do, which turns it into a
  permission slip.
- Treating a screen as a decision. A "go" only means the idea deserves the full process.

## Where its output lands in the Decision Record

The **Screen result** field, and, if the verdict is park, an entry in **Rejected options**
with category (e) deliberately deferred and its revisit trigger.
