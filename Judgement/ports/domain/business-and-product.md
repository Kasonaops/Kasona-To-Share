# Domain pack: business and product

## Fits

Pricing and packaging, product scope and build-versus-validate, a hire, a partnership, a channel
or format, positioning, killing or shrinking something already running. It does not fit a call
about your own career or life (use the personal-life-career pack).

## Horizons

| Kind of decision | Pre-mortem horizon | Default review |
|---|---|---|
| A launch, a price change, a campaign | 3 to 6 months | At 6 weeks and at 6 months |
| A product line or a positioning | 12 to 18 months | At 6 months and at 12 |
| A hire or a partnership | 18 to 24 months | At 90 days and at 12 months |

## Option types to remember

- Do nothing, with the cost of doing nothing written out.
- Test it small first: one segment, one week, one price point.
- Kill or shrink instead of grow.
- Buy or partner instead of build. Build later instead of now.

## MECE buckets for sorting options

- **Offer or price:** by the unit being sold, or by whether the problem being solved is
  functional, financial or trust.
- **Product backlog:** by which assumption each item tests, not by effort or by team.
- **Content or format list:** by the job each piece does, not by channel.
- **Commitments:** by what they scale with (capital, labour, code, media).

## How the biases show up here

| Bias | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| Sunk cost | "We have already built the integration." The build cost is gone either way |
| Confirmation bias | Reading the three positive customer messages and skipping the churn reasons |
| Identity bias | "We are a premium brand", "we are not a discount company", used to end a pricing discussion |
| Social desirability | A competitor or a peer shipped it and it would be uncomfortable to be the one who did not. The Elephant in most business pre-mortems |
| Anchoring on one number | One metric (sign-ups, revenue, followers) crowding out retention, margin or trust |
| Loss aversion | Refusing to retire a feature nobody uses because it feels like a loss |
| Status quo bias | Keeping the current channel or tool because switching needs work |

## Consequences that get overlooked

- Effect on customers and the team currently attached to the thing. A business kill differs from
  a personal one: you cannot exit a relationship at a market price.
- Support load, delivery time and error rate after the change.
- Attention and capacity taken from what already works.
- What the change does to what you want to be known for.

## Hard limits

| Topic | The engine can | The engine cannot, and who to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Legal and contractual (terms, IP, employment law, data protection) | Structure the decision, flag the question as must-know | No legal advice. A qualified lawyer |
| Tax, accounting and financing | Raise the question, flag it as a gap | No tax or financial advice. A tax advisor or accountant |
| Regulated claims (health, finance, safety) | Flag that a claim needs review | No approval of a claim. The responsible professional |
| People decisions with legal weight (dismissal, discrimination) | Structure the options and consequences | Does not replace HR or legal counsel |

## Translation notes

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Option | A concrete path with a budget, an owner and a date |
| Assumption | What must be true for the plan to work. The riskiest one is tested first |
| Concentration | Too much of the roadmap, budget or calendar on one thing |
| Exit trigger | A floor written down before it broke: margin, churn, support load, delivery time |
| Circle of competence | Can the team explain how this works and why it wins, in two plain sentences |
