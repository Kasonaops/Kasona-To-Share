# Multi-Persona Debate

Moderate a structured debate between three or four personas with genuinely, fundamentally
different philosophies. Trading opposing positions back and forth surfaces a more robust
synthesis than any single perspective would reach on its own.

Instead of trying to hold several perspectives in your own head at once, let multiple AI
personas argue the problem out loud while you observe. The points where opposing schools of
thought unexpectedly agree are usually the most robust part of the answer.

This is most useful for complex strategic decisions that don't have a clean, obviously
correct answer, weighing growth strategies, business models, or organizational approaches
against each other. It's equally useful applied to an investment decision: which position to
trim, whether a thesis still holds, or whether a new buy is actually differentiated conviction
or borrowed conviction.

## Example scenario

Say you're deciding how to scale a consulting business. Three personas debate it: "hire
slowly, scale quality," "productize fast, scale systems," and "grow through partnerships."
After four rounds, the synthesis shows all three agree the first five clients need to be
handled personally, the disagreement is only about what happens after that. The debate didn't
resolve the strategic question outright, but it isolated exactly where the real disagreement
lives, which is often more useful than a premature answer.

## Description

Moderates a structured debate between experts with fundamentally different philosophies.
Trading opposing positions surfaces a synthesis that's more robust than any single view.

## Input

- **Debate topic**: the strategic question or decision (required).
- **Three or four debaters**, each with a stated position, background, and core conviction
  (required). If the topic involves reading live market or chart data, one persona can carry
  that role explicitly, but this isn't a requirement of the format itself; assign personas
  based on what the specific decision actually needs.
- **Context** on the decision situation (optional).

## Output

- Round 1: opening statements from all personas.
- Round 2: rebuttal, each persona attacks the others' weak points.
- Round 3: defense and refinement.
- Round 4: common ground, where do all sides unexpectedly agree?
- Synthesis: overlaps, a hybrid solution, and the most robust strategy overall.
- Observation summary: new insights and blind spots the debate revealed.

## Process

### Step 1: Understand the positions

Capture the debate topic and each persona's core convictions clearly before starting.

### Step 2: Opening statements (Round 1)

Each persona presents their position and strongest arguments.

### Step 3: Rebuttal and defense (Rounds 2 and 3)

Each persona attacks the others' weak points with concrete counter-arguments. Then each
responds to the criticism and reinforces their own position.

### Step 4: Common ground and synthesis (Round 4)

Where do all three surprisingly agree? What principles does everyone accept? What hybrid
solution would satisfy all three reasonably well? What's the most robust strategy that emerges?

### Step 5: Observation summary

What became visible that wasn't visible before? What blind spots did the debate expose?

## Definition of done

- [ ] All four debate rounds are run in full.
- [ ] Every position is represented fairly and as strongly as possible.
- [ ] Concrete synthesis points are identified, not just "everyone made good points."
- [ ] A hybrid solution is stated explicitly.
- [ ] Blind spots and new insights are named, not left implicit.

## A note on fairness

The one hard constraint on this format: no position gets to win by default. If you find
yourself writing a weaker version of the persona you disagree with, the debate has stopped
doing its job. The value comes specifically from forcing the strongest possible version of
every side onto the table at once.
