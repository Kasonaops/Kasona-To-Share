# The Push-Mode Digest, an Optional Companion

The board is pull mode: you bring a question, a lens answers. This is the other half, and it is
optional. If you never build it, the board still works.

Push mode is for the people and sources you want to notice without asking: operators a step or
two ahead of you, in your domain or adjacent to it. You do not consult them. You watch what they
demonstrably do, and it arrives on a schedule.

**The hard rule carries over: a watched operator is never simulated.** There is no lens for them.
No output ever says "here is what they would tell you." You record what they shipped, decided, or
said, and nothing more.

## The loop

1. **Curate a small list. The curation is the filter.** This is the whole design. It is tempting
   to monitor a topic, a keyword, or a whole feed, and the result is volume you stop reading
   within three weeks. Ten sources you chose deliberately, each with a written reason for being
   on the list, produce a digest you actually open. The filtering happened when you picked them,
   which is why the digest does not need a scoring model to be useful.

2. **Ingest on a schedule.** Weekly is usually right. Whatever collector fits each source: a
   feed, a channel list, a page monitor, or a manual check on a recurring reminder. Unattended,
   with an alert when it breaks.

3. **Extract the same fields every time.** Comparability is what turns a stack of observations
   into a direction of travel. A workable field set:
   - What did they actually do or decide? Not what they taught or explained. What changed in
     their product, pricing, hiring, or public commitments. If the source is commentary with no
     visible action in it, this is empty, and that is a signal the source is weak, not a reason
     to invent something.
   - Direction of travel, versus roughly three months ago. Needs history. On a first observation,
     leave it empty rather than infer a trend from one point.
   - Positioning shifts: how they present themselves or their work.
   - Product or technology moves.
   - Pass-along value: books, tools, habits, or framings they recommended or visibly modelled.

   Leave a field empty rather than force it. Zero or one strong field beats five padded ones.

4. **Draft a periodic digest.** Short. Grouped by source or by theme. Every entry says what was
   observed and where.

5. **Put a human gate between the digest and anything that gets built.** See below.

## The human gate

The digest does not know what you already have, what you already decided and rejected, or what
matters this month. It cannot know, it only sees the outside world. Wiring it straight into a
backlog produces a queue of other people's priorities.

So every item gets a decision, from a person, with five available outcomes:

| Outcome | When |
|---|---|
| **Build now** | It is genuinely better than what you had planned for this week |
| **Queue with a priority** | Worth doing, not now, and the priority is stated so it is not a graveyard |
| **Park as a not-now idea** | Interesting, no current use, keep the record so a later signal can revive it |
| **Enrich** | Something in this direction already exists, so this improves that instead of starting a new thing |
| **Drop** | It does not apply to you, and saying so out loud stops it coming back every quarter |

"Enrich" is the outcome most setups are missing, and it is where the value is. Most external
signal is not a new thing to build, it is a better version of something you already have.

## The honest caveat, required in every entry

Every watched operator runs a different business than yours. Some of what they optimise for
transfers. Most does not. Copying a tactic without checking whether the underlying model matches
is the main failure mode of watching people.

**Every entry must state both what transfers and what does not.** Never list only the upside. An
entry with no downside column is a sign the caveat was not applied, not that this operator is an
exception.

## Retiring a source

Retire when the reason you added it stops holding: the direction of travel goes flat, their
business diverges too far from anything relevant to you, or your own priorities moved. Move the
entry to a retired section rather than deleting it, so the earlier reasoning survives.

## The anti-role-model slot, optional and often empty

The inverse of a watched operator: a path you deliberately do not want. Usually more useful as an
archetype than as a person, the operator who won the audience and lost the credibility, the
founder who scaled headcount into a business they no longer find interesting. Its job is to make
a failure mode concrete enough to notice early, because "do not become that" is easier to act on
than an abstract principle.

An empty slot is fine. A forced entry is worse than none.
